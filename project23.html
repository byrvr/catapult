🎯 The Big Idea (in one breath)

Imagine your lung is a giant LEGO city. Some streets are healthy and tidy, some are messy and sick. Right now, doctors can take a photo of the city, but a computer can only say "this block is good, that block is bad" — like coloring with only 2 crayons. This project teaches the computer to use all the crayons, so it can see the slow change from a tidy street into a messy one, block by block. That helps scientists understand lung diseases and find better medicine. 🦸

The grown‑up version

Modern machines can map every gene "switch" inside a real piece of lung tissue without breaking it apart. But today's software is clumsy: it chops the tissue into hard, separate labels and misses the gradual gradients — the smooth transitions where one tissue type blends into another. This project builds new deep‑learning AI that captures those smooth transitions and turns them into free, open‑source tools any scientist can reuse.

🤔 The Problem We're Solving

The fancy lung scanner gives us a mountain of clues 🏔️ about every tiny cell. But the computer reading those clues is a bit short‑sighted: it only looks at one spot at a time, so it misses the big picture and the slow "fade" from healthy to sick. It's like trying to understand a sunset by looking at one single pixel. 🌅
  • High‑plex spatial transcriptomics gives single‑cell‑resolution gene expression inside intact tissue.
  • Today's tools mostly use convolutional neural networks (CNNs) or simple clustering.
  • Those methods struggle with long‑range dependencies, irregular cell relationships, and continuous transitions across tissue borders.
  • Result: rich, smooth biology gets squashed into hard discrete labels — hiding the gradual molecular gradients.
🧠 The Hypothesis (the team's bet): tissue borders are actually smooth "transition manifolds," not sharp lines. AI that combines where genes are, what the tissue looks like, and which cells are neighbours will model the lung far better than old CNNs.

💡 The Solution (the clever fix)

Instead of one short‑sighted helper, we build a team of smart AI helpers who each notice something different — colors, shapes, and which cells are friends — and then share notes to draw the best possible map. 🗺️✨
🛰️ See far away
Use state‑space + attention models (like Mamba/Transformers) so the AI notices things that are far apart, not just nearby.
🧬 Mix the clues
Fuse gene locations, cell‑state info, and the tissue's look (H&E histology) into one shared picture.
🕸️ Map the friendships
Graph neural networks treat each cell as a dot, linked to its neighbours — capturing the whole social network of the tissue.
🌈 Catch the fade
Model continuous gradients so we see the smooth change across borders instead of hard on/off labels.

🧩 The 4 Aims (the to‑do list)

Every big quest has steps. Here are the four levels of the game 🎮 — beat them in order and you win!
  • Aim 1 — Build the smart eyes. Create state‑space & attention segmentation models (building on CAMS) that see long‑range patterns, and blend coordinates, cell states, morphology & expert notes into one representation. Compare ("benchmark") against older methods.
  • Aim 2 — Map the friendships. Build spatial graph neural networks where tissue is a cell‑level graph (links = spatial closeness + molecular similarity) to capture higher‑order neighbourhood structure.
  • Aim 3 — Watch the fade. Create continuous spatial gradient models (exploring neural differential equations or diffusion) to measure how strong a gradient is and how regions transition.
  • Aim 4 — Share the toolbox. Deliver modular, version‑controlled, open‑source software that follows FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable).

⚙️ How It Actually Works

First we tidy up the data (no messy clues allowed). Then the AI looks at the picture and the genes together, draws a web of cell‑friends, and finally colors in the smooth rainbow of change. And it even explains its thinking so scientists can trust it! 🌈🔍

🧹 Step 1 — Preprocessing (tidy the data)

  • Strict quality control, aligning spatial coordinates, normalizing gene expression.
  • Build a spatial adjacency graph (who's next to whom).
  • Split data by sample into train / validation / test sets so the AI can't "cheat" (no information leakage).

👀 Step 2 — Segmentation (see the regions)

State‑space & attention models fuse transcript features with the H&E look using cross‑attention — letting genes and pictures "talk" to each other.

🕸️ Step 3 — Graph & Gradient modelling

Graph neural networks map irregular cell interactions (going beyond pixel grids). Continuous embedding spaces replace hard region labels, so gradual change is tracked smoothly.

🔍 Step 4 — Interpretability (show your work)

Built right into the graph model (building on the ProtoPathway approach) to spot characteristic cellular "neighbourhood motifs" — instead of only explaining after the fact.

📖 Word Helper (big words, tiny meanings)

Spatial transcriptomics
A way to see which genes are "on" and exactly where they are inside a tissue.
High‑plex
Measuring lots of things at once (many genes together).
Segmentation
Drawing outlines around the different regions in an image — like coloring inside the lines.
Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)
An older AI good at nearby patterns, but short‑sighted about far‑apart ones.
Attention / Transformer
AI that learns which parts to "pay attention" to, even far away.
State‑space model (Mamba)
A newer AI that handles long sequences efficiently — great for long‑range patterns.
Graph Neural Network (GNN)
AI that works on dots‑and‑lines (cells and their neighbours), like a social network.
Gradient / transition manifold
A smooth fade from one state to another, instead of a hard switch.
H&E histology
A classic stained photo of tissue that shows its shape and structure.
Xenium (10x Genomics)
The machine/platform that produces the high‑plex spatial data.
FFPE
"Formalin‑Fixed Paraffin‑Embedded" — a standard way to preserve tissue samples.
COPD / IPF
Serious lung diseases (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease / idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis).
FAIR principles
Software/data that is Findable, Accessible, Interoperable & Reusable.
CASE project
A PhD done in partnership with a company (here, AstraZeneca), with an industry placement.
